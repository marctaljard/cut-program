01 Split

Rolling 6-session block. No reset day — day 7 is just the next session.

Lower A → Upper A → Lower B → Upper B → Lower C → Upper C → repeat
  • Each muscle: ~3.5× / week
  • Per session: 2–5 sets per muscle
  • Per week: 12–15 direct sets per muscle

02 Sessions

RIR = reps left in the tank. Tuned for hypertrophy, so compounds sit in the 6–12 range at 1–2 RIR rather than heavy triples — same growth, far less joint and systemic cost at seven sessions a week. Rest 2.5–3 min on the first two exercises, 90 sec after. Warm up the first lift with 2–3 ramping sets to your working weight; accessories need none. Type your working weight and last reps into each row — they save automatically.

Lower days have fewer exercises because the lower body has fewer separate targets (quads, hams, glutes, calves) while upper has seven. Per muscle, quads at 14 sets are the second-highest in the programme. Arms count direct sets only — 25+ pressing and pulling sets sit on top. Lagging at week 4? Add one set each to Upper A and Upper C. No more.

Why volume is not higher. More sets do mean more growth, with no clear plateau in the meta-regressions — but that assumes you can recover from them. In a 500 kcal deficit at seven sessions a week you cannot, so the quality levers are doing the work instead: long muscle lengths, 1–2 RIR, 3.5× frequency. Push volume to 18–20 sets on your priority muscles in the gaining phase, not now.

Deliberately not programmed: forearms (covered by pull-ups, rows and heavy DB RDLs — if grip caps your RDL, use straps rather than adding grip work), direct glutes (squats, RDLs, hack and lunges are enough), neck, and rotator cuff. If the shoulder keeps complaining, 2 sets of cable external rotation a week is cheap insurance.

04 Progression

  1. Pick a load you can hit at the bottom of the rep range at target RIR
  2. Add reps weekly until you reach the top of the range on every set
  3. Only then add load (+2.5 kg upper, +5 kg lower) and drop back to the bottom
  4. Reps before load, always. Chasing weight is how the rep range quietly becomes a triple

In a deficit, holding a lift is a win.

Expect machines and accessories to climb and the big compounds to hold. That’s success, not failure.

This is tuned for size, not maximal strength. Squat and bench appear about 1.2 times a week, which is plenty for hypertrophy but low for strength, where the lift is partly a skill needing 2–3 exposures. That trade is deliberate. Your 1RMs will lag what your muscle mass suggests — if you ever want the numbers, spend 4–6 weeks in the 4–6 rep range after the cut and they will catch up quickly.

05 Fatigue rules

No rest days, so this is how fatigue gets managed. Check before each session.

Flag
Lift down 2 sessions running
Under 6.5 h sleep
Joint sharp, not sore
Resting HR +7 bpm
Zero desire to train
FlagsAction
1Train normally
2Drop last 2 exercises, all 3 RIR, skip cardio
3+As above + take the day off

The 3-flag rule is a stop-loss, not a rest day. Expect to use it about twice in 12 weeks. Weekly use means drop Lower C from the rotation.

Deload — weeks 7 and 13. Half the sets, same loads, everything at 4 RIR, two easy low-impact sessions and no run. Still train all 7 days.

06 Cardio

Attached toSessionTime
Upper ABike intervals30 min
Upper BThe run, ~5k—
Upper CZone 2, low-impact45–50 min

Placement is ordered by what follows it, because lifting is the priority. Zone 2 is the softest session, so it sits before Lower A and your heavy squats. The run causes eccentric damage, so it sits before Lower C, the lightest leg day. Intervals cost metabolic fatigue but no muscle damage, so they take the middle slot before Lower B. If your squat starts sliding, shorten the zone 2 before you touch anything in the gym.

The run — alternate each block

  • A: steady 5k, controlled. Don’t race it.
  • B: 5 × 1 km at goal pace, 90 sec walk. Or 6 × 800 m.

Bike intervals

6 × 2 min hard / 2 min easy. Or 8 × 1 min / 90 sec. Build to 8 × 2 min.

Low-impact options, best first

  1. Cross trainer — closest to running
  2. Incline treadmill walk (12–15%, 5–6 km/h) — most knee-friendly, doubles as steps
  3. Bike — best for intervals, zero interference with lifting

Rules

  • Lift first if same day. Separate by 6 h+ where possible
  • Never run before a lower session
  • Low-impact runs 25–30% longer than the run it replaces
  • Zone 2 = full sentences, no gasping
  • Test 5k at weeks 1, 6 and 12

Expect: sub-26 very likely. Sub-25 possible but not guaranteed on one run a week. Add the second run after the cut.
